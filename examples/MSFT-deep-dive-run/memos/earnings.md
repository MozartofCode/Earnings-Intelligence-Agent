## Bottom line
At the operating line, earnings power is accelerating. Constant-currency revenue growth went 15% → 19% over three quarters, Azure is at +48% (guided to 49–50%), and FY27 Q1 operating margin was 49.8% [D5][D2][E7]. Revenue has landed above the top of the guided range 4 of 4 times. The counterweight is that commercial RPO has been flat at ~$625–678B since FY26 Q2 and FY27 Q1 bookings fell 12% [E2][D2][E7].

## Key findings
1. **Revenue is accelerating in constant currency.** Constant-currency y/y went +17% / +15% / +15% / +17% / +19% from FY26 Q1 to FY27 Q1; reported was +16.7% to +18.8%. The Q2 FY27 guide of $96.0–97.1B means +18–19% [D5][E1][E2][E3][D4][D2][E7].
2. **Incremental margins are high.** Y/y Δ operating income / Δ revenue was 56.9% / 49.9% / 46.3% / 54.5% from FY26 Q2 to FY27 Q1. FY27 Q1 opex rose +6% while headcount fell 2% [D5][D1].
3. **Management sandbags revenue.** Revenue landed above the top of the guided range 4 of 4 times, and Azure beat its constant-currency guide by 1–4 pts each time (Table 2) [E5][E9][E6][E4].
4. **The FY27 Q1 beat was operating, not below the line.** Revenue was $92.28B vs a $89.85–90.95B guide. Opex was $16.64B vs $16.8–16.9B. COGS was inside its range. The tax rate was 20.3% vs ~20% guided, a small headwind. Non-GAAP EPS was $4.89 vs a $4.72 web consensus [D5][E4][D12].
5. **Non-GAAP EPS growth slowed to +18%** from +21% to +24%, even though operating income grew +21%. A weaker ex-OpenAI other income line and the higher tax rate explain the gap (est.). FY26 Q4 non-GAAP EPS of $4.74 includes a $3.2B pre-tax Anthropic gain, because the adjustment strips out OpenAI only [E1][E2][E3][D4][E4].
6. **The leading indicators have stalled.** Commercial RPO went $392B → $625B → $627B → $678B → $677B. RPO ex-OpenAI grew +25% in FY26 Q4; that figure is not found for FY27 Q1. FY27 Q1 bookings were −12% (automated transcript) [E3][E2][E1][E4][D2][E7].
7. **Capex guidance is weaker than revenue guidance, and its definition changed.** FY27 Q1 capex was about $44.6B ($39.4B cash PP&E plus $5.19B finance-lease ROU) vs an "over $50 billion" guide; management cited delayed third-party capacity. Datacenter useful lives went from 15 to 25 years in FY27, which shifts leases from finance to operating and out of "capex." Calendar-2026 capex guidance went ~$190B → ~$175B → ~$170B. Depreciation still rose to $11.1B from $7.1B [D1][E4][E6][E7][E8].

## Judgment
Judgment: The revenue engine is accelerating and guidance is a reliable floor. Each Azure guide has been beaten and then set above the prior actual, the profile of a supply-constrained business catching up with demand. Incremental margins of about 50% at this scale, with opex below guide, are high quality.

Judgment: Three things hold the score at 4. (a) RPO and bookings have stalled. If ex-OpenAI demand is slowing, the Azure acceleration has a shelf life. (b) EPS is growing more slowly than operating income, and the Q2 guide implies a slightly lower y/y margin. (c) Accounting choices flatter the optics: the useful-life extension (not quantified), the move to operating leases (lower reported capex), and a non-GAAP measure that keeps Anthropic gains. None of this looks like manipulation, but it makes operating income the cleaner gauge.

Judgment, on call tone: the July promise that H1 would accelerate was kept [E4]. The October call adds new caveats: delayed third-party capacity, GPU contracts pushed into H2, "material volatility in bookings," and Windows OEM below expectations. The one captured Q&A answer on Azure returns gave no numbers [E7]. Specificity held on revenue but fell on capex and bookings. This rests on an automated transcript only. Beats against consensus are measurable only for FY27 Q1, so beat quality rests on beats against guidance.

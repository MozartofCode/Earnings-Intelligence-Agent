# Bear case · MSFT · 2026-11-18 · price $508.29 [D6]

## Thesis
The price assumes the AI build-out earns software-like returns, but its biggest buyer (OpenAI: 10.8% of revenue and, by my estimate, at least a third of commercial RPO) is no longer captive, and fully loaded FCF is already down to ~$13–23B a year. The bear case is right if FY27 Q2–Q3 show RPO below ~$700B, OpenAI revenue below $10.0B a quarter, and lease-loaded FCF margin under 10%.

## What the market is missing
**Consensus:** Azure +48%, a $677B RPO and four straight beats make the capex self-funding, so the FCF trough is temporary [E4][D2].

**Bear view:** backlog and capex are concentrated in a few AI labs (counterparty and pricing risk, not annuity demand). It is hidden because:
- The FY27 recast merges M365 commercial (the moat) with Azure in "Agents and Infra" [D2][I3].
- The useful-life change (15 to 25 years) moves future leases from finance to operating, so reported capex falls with no change in cash [E4].
- Headline FCF ($60.0B TTM) excludes finance-lease capacity and component prepayments [F-Table 2].

## Pillars

**1. The backlog depends on one non-exclusive counterparty, and it has stalled.**
- **Evidence:**
  - FY26 Q4 commercial RPO grew +84%, but only +25% ex-OpenAI [E4]. That implies OpenAI RPO of at least ~$218B, ≥32% of $678B (est., lower bound). Secondary reports say 45% [BE3].
  - RPO has been flat for three quarters ($625B → $627B → $678B → $677B), and FY27 Q1 bookings fell 12% [E7].
  - OpenAI: $10.0B revenue; receivable $6.0B → $7.2B in one quarter [D1].
  - Since 2026-04-27 OpenAI can buy from any cloud; it has reported commitments of ~$300B with Oracle and ~$138B with AWS [I12][I17].
  - New 10-Q RPO language: "potential renegotiation of commitments" [D1][F2].
  - Reports (allegations) say OpenAI cut its compute plan to ~$600B from ~$1.4T [BE3].
- **Mechanism:** RPO slips or is renegotiated; Azure fades toward market growth; single-tenant capacity is resold cheaper.
- **Early warning:** FY27 Q2 10-Q (late Jan 2027): OpenAI revenue below $8B or its receivable outgrowing revenue; RPO at or below ~$680B.

**2. Reported FCF overstates cash, increasingly.**
- **Evidence:**
  - FY26 FCF: $67.0B reported, ~$22.5B (est.) after $24.6B of finance-lease adds and a $19.9B component outflow; TTM ~$12.7B (est.) vs $142.6B net income [F1][F-Table 2].
  - Component receivables went from $8.2B to $35.7B in five quarters [F2].
  - Uncommenced leases: $364.6B (77% of equity), through FY2034 [D1].
  - Quarterly depreciation $11.1B vs $7.1B a year ago [D1].
  - $25B of new bonds priced 2026-10-29 (4.85–6.35% fixed), the first large USD deal since 2017 [BE2][I18].
- **Mechanism:** depreciation and lease costs catch up with ~$160B a year of capacity spend and compress the 49.8% operating margin.
- **Early warning:** FY27 Q2 component receivables above $45B [F]; depreciation outgrowing revenue.

**3. The price needs margins Microsoft has never earned post-SBC, on a lease-loaded basis.**
- **Evidence:**
  - Break-even year-5 post-SBC margin is 31.1% at 19% growth, above the best year in the data (29.1%, FY22), before ~6% of revenue in finance leases [V3][V7].
  - My lease-loaded reverse DCF (start 7.7% margin; lease liabilities in net debt) needs 35% year-1 growth at a 20% margin, or a 45% margin at 15% growth [BE1].
- **Mechanism:** the multiple compresses toward peer EV/EBIT (21.1x vs 23.0x) as margins roll over [V4].
- **Early warning:** FY27 Q2 guide-implied operating margin is ~46.8%, down y/y [E-Table 3].

**4. AI-infrastructure structure is worsening; Microsoft is losing share.**
- **Evidence:**
  - Synergy (est.): share 20% → 19%; market +55% vs Azure +48% [I11].
  - Microsoft Cloud gross margin 66%; company 67.7% vs 69.8% in FY24 [I2][D5].
  - Oracle's $664B RPO sits on the same few AI labs [I8].
- **Mechanism:** price competition on commodity GPU capacity against strong suppliers.
- **Early warning:** Synergy Q4 2026 share (~Feb 2027); Microsoft Cloud gross margin below 65%.

## Steelman of the bull
**Their best argument:** the moat is M365: +17% revenue on +5% seats, Copilot at ~6.7% of seats, RPO ex-OpenAI +25%, and two-thirds of capex is short-lived GPUs management can "slow down" [I2][I14][E4]. All sequential FY26 Q4 RPO growth came from non-frontier-lab customers [E4]. Downside is a capex pause, not stranded assets.

**My answer:** I accept the M365 pricing power; it is why the bear value is $226, not lower. That sequential gain was $51B, and RPO then went flat [E7]. "Slow down" does not cover $364.6B of signed leases or $35.7B of component prepayments [D1][F2], and ex-OpenAI RPO was disclosed for FY26 Q4 but not FY27 Q1 [E-OQ]. **Admission:** if ex-OpenAI RPO keeps growing at +25% or faster, Pillar 1 weakens sharply.

## What would make me abandon this case
1. FY27 Q2 RPO above ~$720B with ex-OpenAI growth of +25% or more.
2. OpenAI revenue holding at $10B a quarter or more while its receivable falls below $6B.
3. Component receivables shrinking and two quarters of lease-loaded post-SBC FCF margin above 15%.

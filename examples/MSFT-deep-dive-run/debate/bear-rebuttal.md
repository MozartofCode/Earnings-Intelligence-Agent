# Bear rebuttal · MSFT · 2026-11-18 · price $508.29 [D6]

## Bull Pillar 1: Demand exceeds supply, so guidance is a floor — **Reframe**
- **Fact accepted:** revenue beat the top of guidance 4 of 4 times, Azure beat its constant-currency guide by 1–4 pts, and the FY27 Q2 guide is 49–50% [E-T2][E7]. I concede near-term revenue: a miss at FY27 Q2 is unlikely, so my first trigger is a backlog trigger, not a revenue miss.
- **Wrong implication:** supply-constrained revenue tells us about *current* capacity, not *duration*. The forward indicators have stalled:
  - RPO: $625B → $627B → $678B → $677B [E-F6].
  - FY27 Q1 bookings: −12%; management now warns of "material volatility in bookings" [E7].
  - Ex-OpenAI RPO was disclosed for FY26 Q4 (+25%) and not for FY27 Q1 [E-F6].
- **The bull's own condition:** it says the capex shortfall ($44.6B vs "over $50B") must be a delay, not weaker demand [E-F7]. Both readings fit a quarter where bookings fell, so the condition is untested.

## Bull Pillar 2: Operating leverage shows the capex is paying back — **Contest**
- **The 49.8% comparison is seasonal.** FY26 Q1 printed 48.9%, then 47.1%, 46.3% and 45.1% through the year [E-T1]. Q1 carries the lowest opex: $16.64B, vs $18.5–18.6B guided for Q2 [E-T2].
- **The bull's own figure shows the slide.** Guide-implied FY27 Q2 margin is ~46.8% vs 47.1% a year ago, down y/y [E-T3].
- **Opex is doing the work, not cloud gross margin.** Headcount fell 2% [E-F2], while Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell to 66% and company gross margin to 67.7% (69.8% in FY24) [I2][D5].
- **The thesis claim fails on a cash basis.** The thesis says capex "already covers its depreciation." D&A is $38.5B plus $5.4B of lease amortization, against ~$160B of FY26 capacity spend [F-F1]. Today's income statement simply has not absorbed that spend yet, and the bull concedes it "cannot answer the lease wave."

## Bull Pillar 3: Capex intensity can bend — **Contest**
- **The guidance cut is mostly a reclassification.** On calendar-2026 capex (~$190B → ~$175B), the CFO said "outside of this useful life impact, our calendar year 2026 CapEx investment expectations remain unchanged." The cut moved datacenter leases from finance to operating, which takes them out of reported capex [E4].
- **The rest looks like slippage.** The further cut to ~$170B coincided with delayed third-party capacity and GPU contracts pushed into H2 [E7][E-F7].
- **"Short-lived" covers only part of the spend.** About two-thirds of capex is CPUs and GPUs [E4]. It does not cover $364.6B of signed, uncommenced leases or $35.7B of component receivables [D1][F-F2].
- The bull itself writes that "no quarter yet shows intensity falling."

## Bull Pillar 4: A switching-cost franchise with pricing power — **Concede** (partly Reframe)
- **Conceded:** M365 commercial cloud grew +17% on +5% seats [I2]. That is why my bear value is $226 and not lower. **Cost to the bear case:** it puts a floor under the productivity profit pool, so a full de-rating to peer EV/EBIT is less likely.
- **Reframe:** the 52.9% segment margin blends M365 with Azure under the FY27 recast, so it does not show what AI infrastructure earns on its own [I3]. Seat comparisons now include GitHub seats [F-F7], and the Copilot seat count was omitted in FY27 Q1 [BU2].

## Valuation note (bull's own numbers)
- The bull gets **$605 (+19%) only on a 15-year fade**. With the same inputs on 10 years it gets **$443 (−13%)** [BU1].
- The valuation base case is **$403 (−21%)** [V3].
- Even the bull's preferred horizon leaves the stock below fair value on a 10-year view.

Updated probability: 30% (judgment) that the bear scenario (~$226, 10-year horizon, lease-loaded) plays out. The near-term revenue and M365 concessions keep it below one in three.

Deciding question: At the FY27 Q2 print (~2027-01-27), is ex-OpenAI commercial RPO still growing at +25% or more while capex plus finance-lease additions fall as a share of revenue?
